## 🎬 Demo Script (5-7 minutes)

### Part 1: The Problem (60 seconds)

**Say:**
> "Let me show you a project I built that demonstrates how I think about AI and product development. It's called Speed Rail.
>
> The problem it solves is simple but painful: designers constantly waste time searching for approved copy. They'll spend 15 minutes digging through Notion docs trying to figure out if they should use 'Sign In' or 'Log In' or 'Login.' And even after all that, unapproved copy still slips through to production because there's no validation.
>
> This creates bottlenecks—content designers become the blocker for every design review. And it creates risk—inconsistent copy ships to users."

**Why this works:** You're framing a real, relatable problem that every product team faces.

---

### Part 2: The Solution Overview (60 seconds)

**Say:**
> "Speed Rail is an AI-powered content database that lives directly in Figma. It solves three problems:
>
> 1. **Discovery**: Designers get instant access to approved terms—no searching
> 2. **Validation**: It automatically scans designs and highlights unapproved copy in red
> 3. **Productivity**: It generates dozens of design variations from templates in seconds
>
> Think of it like a design system, but for words. Or like Grammarly meets your design system."

**Screen share:** Show the README.md with the feature list visible.

**Why this works:** Clear, concise value prop with familiar comparisons (design system, Grammarly).

---

### Part 3: Live Demo (3 minutes)

#### Demo Step 1: Show the Database (30 seconds)

**Do:**
- Switch to Airtable browser tab
- Show the database with approved terms
- Point out the simple structure: Term, Platform, Explanation

**Say:**
> "It connects to Airtable—which is important because non-technical content designers can manage this themselves. No developer needed to add or update terms. This is the source of truth."

**Why this works:** Shows you thought about the user (content designers) and their needs (no code).

---

#### Demo Step 2: Browse Terms in Figma (30 seconds)

**Do:**
- Switch to Figma
- Open the Speed Rail plugin
- Show the list of approved terms
- Filter by platform (iOS, Android, etc.)
- Click a term to insert it on canvas

**Say:**
> "In Figma, designers just open the plugin, browse approved terms, filter by platform if needed, and click to insert. Five seconds instead of 15 minutes searching docs."

**Why this works:** Concrete time savings, simple UX.

---

#### Demo Step 3: Content Validation (60 seconds)

**Do:**
- Select a frame with some approved and some unapproved terms
- Click "Scan Frame for Invalid Terms"
- Show unapproved terms highlighted in red

**Say:**
> "Here's the magic: validation. I select any frame, click scan, and it automatically highlights unapproved terms in red. This catches mistakes before they ship.
>
> Imagine running this before every design review—you'd never ship inconsistent copy again. It's like a spell-checker, but for your design system."

**Why this works:** Visual, immediate impact. "Before they ship" = risk mitigation.

---

#### Demo Step 4: Mock Generation - The Killer Feature (60 seconds)

**Do:**
- Select a template frame with placeholder text ({{Content}} or {{Zone Name}})
- Click "Generate Mocks from Template"
- Watch as 10-20 variations are created instantly
- Zoom out to show them all

**Say:**
> "And here's the feature no competitor has: mock generation. I create one template with a placeholder—see the {{Content}} tag here—select it, click generate, and boom. Twenty variations in two seconds.
>
> This used to take an hour of manual copy-pasting. Now it's instant. This is a 30x productivity boost for designers working on variants—button states, empty states, multi-language designs, you name it."

**Why this works:**
- Visual wow factor
- Clear, quantifiable productivity win (30x)
- Unique differentiator

---

### Part 4: The AI Vision (60 seconds)

**Switch to ROADMAP.md**

**Say:**
> "Now here's where AI comes in. This is version 1—it works, it's useful, teams would pay for it today. But the roadmap is where it gets exciting.
>
> **[Point to Phase 1: AI Intelligence]**
>
> Next phase: AI-powered content suggestions. Instead of browsing a list, the AI analyzes your design context—'Oh, this is a button in a checkout flow'—and suggests the most appropriate approved term. Like Copilot, but for copy.
>
> Then AI content generation: if no approved term exists, the AI generates new suggestions that match your brand voice, flags them for approval, and learns from your feedback.
>
> This is how you scale—AI handles the repetitive pattern matching, humans handle the creative decisions."

**Why this works:** Shows you're thinking about AI as a multiplier, not a replacement. Shows product vision beyond v1.

---

### Part 5: The Business Case (60 seconds)

**Switch to COMPETITIVE_ANALYSIS.md (or just reference it)**

**Say:**
> "I also did competitive analysis. The main competitors are Frontitude and Writer.com.
>
> **Frontitude** is enterprise-focused—$50-100 per user per month, takes 2-4 weeks to set up, complex. Great for localization at scale, but overkill for most teams.
>
> **Writer.com** is an AI writing assistant—$18-40 per user per month, grammar and style checking, but not design-specific. No mock generation, not Figma-native.
>
> **Speed Rail's positioning:** 75% cheaper, 100x faster setup, design-first, with a unique killer feature (mock generation).
>
> Pricing model: Free tier for individuals, $15/month for pro, $49/month for teams. The market is there—every design team with 5+ people has this problem. That's tens of thousands of potential customers."

**Why this works:** Shows market awareness, strategic thinking, competitive positioning. You're not just building—you're thinking about how to win.

---

### Part 6: The Meta Point (30 seconds)

**Say:**
> "So why am I showing you this? Because this is how I work:
>
> 1. **I identify real problems** by understanding user pain points
> 2. **I build working solutions** - this plugin actually works in production
> 3. **I leverage AI strategically** - not just slapping GPT on everything, but thoughtful integration
> 4. **I think commercially** - market size, pricing, competition
>
> And this is exactly how I'd approach projects at Design Nurse. Understand the problem deeply, prototype fast, leverage AI where it multiplies impact, and always think about the business model.
>
> That's Speed Rail."

**Why this works:** You're connecting the dots explicitly. This isn't just a project—it's a demonstration of your entire approach to product development.

## 💡 Handling Questions

### "Why Airtable? Isn't that limiting?"

**Answer:**
> "Great question. Airtable for v1 is strategic—it lets non-technical users manage the database with zero setup. For v2, the roadmap includes a hosted option for teams that outgrow Airtable. But starting with Airtable means anyone can try this in 5 minutes, which is critical for adoption."

---

### "What about Figma building this natively?"

**Answer:**
> "That's always a risk with platform plugins, but it's also validation—if Figma builds it, it proves the market need. Plus, we have unique features like mock generation and AI content generation that go beyond what a platform typically builds. And worst case, Figma acquires us. [smile]"

---

### "How would you monetize this?"

**Answer:**
> "Freemium model. Free tier for individuals to drive adoption and word-of-mouth. $15/month for pro users, $49/month for teams. The key insight is per-team pricing, not per-seat—makes it affordable for growing teams. Enterprise tier at $500+ for custom infrastructure and SSO. [Pull up pricing table in ROADMAP.md]"

---

### "Why would teams pay for this?"

**Answer:**
> "ROI is simple: if one designer saves 2 hours per month, that's $100-200 in value. Plugin pays for itself immediately. But the real value is risk mitigation—preventing inconsistent copy from shipping is worth way more than $49/month."

---

### "What's the hardest technical challenge?"

**Answer:**
> "The mock generation feature was tricky—detecting placeholders in both text content and layer names, preserving formatting, loading all the fonts used in templates. But the next challenge is AI integration: building context-aware suggestions that actually understand component types, user flows, and design patterns. That's where the real intelligence comes in."

---

### "Have you talked to potential users?"

**Answer:**
> "This was inspired by working with design teams who face this problem daily. The validation is that Frontitude raised $8M solving a similar problem—the market exists. Next step would be beta testing with 5-10 design teams to validate willingness to pay and gather feedback on the AI features."

**Or if you have talked to users:**
> "Yes! I spoke with [X] designers at [Y] companies, and the consistent feedback was: [summarize key insights]. That's what informed the roadmap priorities."

## 🚫 What NOT to Do

### Don't
- ❌ Apologize for what's not built yet ("Sorry, the AI part isn't done yet...")
- ❌ Get too technical ("So the API call uses async/await with error handling...")
- ❌ Downplay the achievement ("It's just a simple plugin...")
- ❌ Wing the demo (practice at least 3x before)
- ❌ Forget to mute notifications

### Do
- ✅ Own what you've built ("This works today, and here's the vision")
- ✅ Stay high-level on technical details (unless they ask)
- ✅ Show confidence ("This solves a real problem that teams will pay for")
- ✅ Practice until smooth
- ✅ Have backup plan if demo breaks (screenshots/video)

You've nailed it if they say:
